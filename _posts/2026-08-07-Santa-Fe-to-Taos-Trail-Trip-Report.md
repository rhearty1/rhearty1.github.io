## Overview

Lauren and I hiked the Santa Fe to Taos Trail northbound from Tuesday, July 21, through Wednesday, July 29, 2026. We began by taking the Blue Bus 300/200 from Taos at 7:55 a.m. to Santa Fe Plaza, arriving around 10:15. After a late breakfast at the French bakery in La Fonda, we started hiking at about 11:15 a.m. Nine days later, we reached the Taos Cow around 12:30 p.m. and celebrated with breakfast burritos.

The trip was a joy. The route was both more remote and more varied than we expected, with aspen groves, blooming wildflowers, open fields, mountain views, canyons, caves, dry and running creeks, and four alpine lakes: Katherine, Stewart, Pecos Baldy, and Truchas. We saw three bighorn rams at close range, cows in Infinity Field, dusky grouse, fence lizards, and plenty of other signs of wildlife. We also met a memorable collection of hikers and locals along the way.

The place that most struck Buckaroo was Puerto Nambe, where he felt he could have camped for many nights. The sound that remains most strongly associated with the trip, though, is from Infinity Field: coyotes howling from dusk to dawn, their calls bouncing off the canyon walls.

## Planning and Expectations

We found the SF2T because Tumblin' Tan had an event to attend in Taos on Saturday, July 18, and we started looking for backpacking trips in the area. That led us to Pam's SF2T project.

Our main planning resources were Pam's guide and GPX file, including the alternate routes she mapped. We eventually used four of those alternates. Our main concerns before starting were water availability and food quantity. Water turned out to be manageable with Pam's information. Food was another story: we carried far too much, and after resupplying we ended up donating much of the excess to an already overflowing hiker box. Tumblin' Tan also had very little appetite for much of the trip.

We expected the climb toward Lake Katherine to be one of the hardest parts because we had not specifically conditioned for the elevation and climbing.

### Significant Route Deviations

We used four alternate routes:

1. **Comales Trail / FR 22:** We used this to avoid ascending to Ripley Point during a thunderstorm.
2. **Ojitos Maes / FR 422:** This substantial redirect avoided poorly marked trails and difficult pathfinding after our zero day at Agua Piedra.
3. **FR 438:** We used the road walk instead of the switchbacks down to Rio Chiquito. This road had been part of SF2T v1.0; our impression was that Pam later changed the route to reduce the amount of road walking.
4. **Drake Canyon alternate / FR 437:** We followed this alternate on the approach toward Taos.

## Conditions and Trail Character

The route varied substantially in how developed it felt. Trails within the Pecos Wilderness were generally well marked, and most forest roads were also reasonably easy to follow. Some overgrown or less obvious sections required more attention, though Pam's route information helped. Our one clear navigation mistake was following a deer path for about an eighth of a mile on the descent toward Agua Piedra before recognizing that we had left the route.

Weather became a defining feature of the middle of the trip. We experienced three consecutive days of thunderstorms and heavy rain, including exposed hiking on Comales Trail and a two-hour downpour during our zero day. The combination of high country, afternoon storms, and long route segments made early starts and flexible routing valuable.

## Notes for Future Hikers

### Navigation

Carry Pam's guide and GPX, and make sure the track is available offline. We used OsmAnd successfully. The main route is often straightforward, but some overgrown or lightly traveled sections demand active navigation. The available alternates were extremely useful when weather, trail condition, or mileage made the primary route unattractive.

### Water

Pam's water information was adequate for us, but expect some dry camps and pay attention to conditions after storms. At Agua Piedra, for example, the pump water tasted strongly of sulfur, while the creek ran clear before the downpour and became badly soiled afterward.

### Food and Resupply

We carried far too much food. Our single resupply added more than we needed, and we gave away much of it. Appetite at altitude and under sustained exertion can be unpredictable, so our experience argues for being conservative about how much food to add at resupply if you are already carrying a surplus.

### Weather

The monsoon pattern mattered. We had three successive days with thunderstorms, including one period when we were exposed and soaked. Early starts and a willingness to take alternates were important.

### Transportation

The Blue Bus 300/200 made a point-to-point hike unusually easy. We used it from Taos to Santa Fe before the hike and again to return to Santa Fe after spending a couple of nights in Taos.

## Overall Impression

The SF2T felt more remote and more varied than we had expected. Its strongest quality was the succession of different landscapes: city edge, mountain forest, alpine lakes, high passes, canyons, creeks, open fields, forest roads, and finally the approach to Taos. It also had enough rough edges - route-finding, long connectors, road walking, weather exposure, and dry stretches - to feel less like a conventional, fully standardized long-distance trail and more like a developing route that rewards flexibility.

That was part of its appeal. The trip gave us both sustained solitude and a surprising amount of trail community, from the hikers we repeatedly crossed paths with to the people we met at campgrounds. If there is one image that captures the route for Buck, it is Infinity Field at night: cows settled in the grass, canyon walls in the dark, and coyotes calling from all sides. 

That's all from me. Now it's time to hear from Tumblin' Tan.

## Day 1 - Santa Fe to Borrego Trail

Beautiful to start in Santa Fe after taking the bus from Taos.

This was a long, hot climb out of the city, first surrounded by beautiful homes, then homes with views, then less and less infrastructure.

It was hot until we reached the Winsor Trail and water at Big Tesuque Creek.

## Day 2 - Borrego to Lake Katherine

We climbed 4,400 feet today!! It was long and hard, yet only 9 miles somehow. Lake Katherine is beautiful and we met another lovely couple, Leslie and Steve, who came out from Texas for a few days.

## Day 3 - Lake Katherine to Jack's Creek Campground

Buckaroo woke up in rough shape today, having worried that we spent the night in the middle of the trail. (We did.) We took the omrning slowly before beginning on a long decline that lasted most of the day.

Towards the end of the evening we arrived at our campsite to find that it wasn't a campsite, only a field with mole hills! Unhappily, we hiked on (especially Tumblin' TAN) until we found a dry creek and creepy campsite that we made work.

## Day 4 - Jack's Creek to Infinity Field

Today was **EPIC**, and I'm not sure I'll ever tell my dad about it. On the way up, we heard the wind-wails of burned trees.

Then we entered the alpine zone and had a beautiful morning at Pecos Baldy, seeing some amazing views.

Then things got a bit strange and difficult. We passed an inflated dead cow on the side of the trail.

Then we turned a corner and there were three huge bighorn sheep. We had been warned that they can be territorial, so we booked it up the side of the mountain to try and get a safe distance from them. After the three rams consulted with each other for longer than I cared to wait, they calmly walked down the trail, past us.

We made it to the next lake, Truchas Lake, and heard someone shouting. He was the first person we had seen in 24 hours. Jeff had lost his dog, Major, who had been swimming in the lake and then ran off. Jeff was very worried and sad, but thankfully, 45 minutes later, Major came running down the path. It was great to see them reunited!

Now it was 1:30 p.m. and we had to get over the divide before the afternoon storm hit. It was beautiful on the top, but that was followed by some scary switchbacks on the other side of the divide.

We made it to the treeline and triumphantly took a break. Unfortunately, our peace was short-lived, as the storm that chased us all day was again right on our heels.

TAN took off hiking with Buck not far behind when a **huge** lightning bolt and thunder hit. Buck caught up with TAN and they took off running, trying to get to lower ground. The storm continued and the pair "hauled ass" down the mountain for 5 miles in the thunderstorm. It was scary.

Funny enough, this crazy day ended in a peaceful cow pasture surrounded by about 20 sleeping cows. Hopefully they don't get too curious about us tonight!

## Day 5 - Infinity Field to Agua Piedra Campground

The cows didn't bother us even once last night, but we did wake up a few times hearing the coyotes yap, yell, and howl. The yaps and howls echoed off the canyon and sounded like we were surrounded by coyotes on all sides.

We had a relaxed morning, hoping to recover from yesterday's adventures.

The hike into Santa Barbara Campground was beautiful, and we arrived on a summer Saturday with lots of people and joy all around. But, as we're coming to learn, all victories are short-lived in the mountains above Santa Fe, and it started to rain about 20 minutes after we arrived.

We took a lunch break under a tree and then began another hard hike up, up, and up! We took the "alternate" route, which was supposed to be easier, but got caught in a huge downpour as we hiked up a steep fire road.

Fearful from our thunder experience the day before, we jumped off the trail to hide under the trees for about 45 minutes.

Once again we were soaked, cold, tired, and climbing. Morale was low and we discussed bailing on the trip. Buck's ankle also wasn't feeling great.

Luckily, we had an easy hike into Agua Piedra Campground and we were spared more storms. The people are kind and curious about our adventure, with one offering to host us in Taos when we arrive on Wednesday!

## Zero Day at Agua Piedra

We are taking a "0" day today to resupply and then will push out three hard days to get there. We are going to plan for storms, aiming to leave early each day and hike on despite the weather. We are tired but hopeful that we can make it to Taos!

## Day 7 - Agua Piedra to Fish Gate  

It didn't rain today and we were so grateful! We finally started early, around 6:20 a.m., and took on the first hill, which was hard.

Then the rest of the day was easy on a fire road. About 4 miles into the 8-mile road walk, we ran into our group of friends we met on the first day and hiked the rest of the day with Rolf, Eric, Elizabeth, and Mary.

Fish Gate was a beautiful campsite and we welcomed the company after a number of days on our own. It was a **20-mile day** for us!

## Day 8 - Fish Gate to Drake Canyon  

This was a lovely 15-mile hike through the Taos forests.

It was beautiful and easy, as it was mostly downhill! Tan was tired from yesterday, but we continued to hike with our new friends, which helped pass the time.

We are 12 miles from Taos and will finish tomorrow!!
