Questions worth exploring

  1. Where do the unexpected gaps come from? Your own key source, Djupedal and colleagues (2025), measured sickness absence as its outcome. A 2025 Southampton study of about 2.5 million shifts linked understaffing in the previous week to more registered nurses reporting sick, and weeks of all long shifts to 26 percent higher odds. If gaps are produced by the roster and the vacancy rate, the call-in decision treats a symptom (R3).
  2. Is this still NATO Project 5? You registered an AI clinical decision tool, moved to shift planning in Week 9, and to call-in approval in Week 10, with no AI and no clinical decision. Does your sponsor know, and agree? If you are leaving the sponsor, say why in one sentence.
  3. Can you get the records? Your replay probe needs a de-identified past call-in and what the coordinator saw. Which hospital, which coordinator, and what permission would you need? Without the records, the probe cannot run.
  4. Is it missing information or missing authority? HSSIB found that managers may not see extra shifts worked elsewhere and may override fatigue warnings. If coordinators already see the warning and approve anyway, better information will not change the choice. Your probe's reading rules can tell these apart; say which result you expect.
  5. Is approval where the choice is made? In many offices the first person reached who says yes gets the shift. The order of calls, made before your approval step, may decide the outcome. Watch one coordinator work a gap.
  6. Why the ship? The Olivia case is vivid, and you note that its mechanism differs from a hospital call-in. A hospital incident would make the same point with the users you mean to serve.
  7. Why do workers say yes? Pay, loyalty, guilt or pressure? The worker is a decision-maker too, and the one whose rest is lost. Talk to two nurses who take extra shifts.

What this map leaves out

  • It shows a hospital. The maritime case you open with has a different chain of command and no bank of spare staff.
  • Agency staff, unions and pay rules are folded into the budget and pool boxes.
  • Patient harm from short staffing is not drawn; it would sit to the right of the journey.
  • I have read the Southampton study only as a press release; check the full paper before relying on the 26 percent figure.
